# Workshop 2 — Risk origins

## What the method requires

Workshop 2 identifies **who** might want to harm the object under study and
**why**:

- a **risk origin** (RO): the actor (state, cybercriminal group, competitor,
  disgruntled employee, hacktivist...);
- a **target objective** (TO): what that actor is after (espionage, financial
  gain, sabotage, destabilisation...);
- the **RO/TO pair** is the unit of work. Its **relevance** is assessed against
  the origin's motivation and resources, then the most relevant pairs are
  **selected** for what follows.

## In the tool

### Start the workshop

**Workshop 2** → **Start workshop**.

### Add an RO/TO pair

**Risk origin / target objective pairs** section → **Add an RO/TO pair**:

- **Risk origin category**: official EBIOS RM list (State, Organised crime,
  Terrorist, Ideological activist, Specialised firm, Amateur, Avenger,
  Pathological attacker, Other).
- **Target objective category**: Espionage, Strategic pre-positioning,
  Influence/destabilisation, Operational disruption, Sabotage/destruction,
  Financial gain, Challenge/fun, Other.
- **Descriptions**: describe the actor and the objective in your context.
- **Typical motivation** (1 to 4) and **resources** (1 to 4) of the origin.

**From the library** offers typical pairs (financially motivated ransomware,
state espionage, sabotage by an internal avenger...). The **→ library** button
on an existing pair capitalises it into your personal library.

### Relevance

The tool computes a **relevance** level from motivation and resources. You can
record a different expert judgement, with justification (the gap stays visible
and "sticky").

### Select the pairs

Mark as **selected** the pairs that will seed the strategic scenarios of
Workshop 3. A pair that is not selected stays recorded but is not propagated.

## Validate

**Validate workshop** requires at least one selected RO/TO pair. The Workshop 2
PDF report lists the pairs, their relevance and the justification of any gaps.

## Tips

- Do not confuse **risk origin** (the actor) with an ecosystem **stakeholder**
  (Workshop 3): a stakeholder may *become* a vector, but it is not the same
  notion.
- Stay realistic: 3 to 6 selected pairs are usually enough for a well-framed
  study.
- Motivation and resources are reasoned **relative to your organisation** (a
  low-visibility target reduces motivation).
